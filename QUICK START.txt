XENO CAPTURE

Xeno Capture is an open-source Windows app for previewing and recording
HDMI video from a capture card. It supports windowed or fullscreen viewing,
PC audio monitoring, and local MP4 recordings and PNG snapshots. Keep the
controller connected to the console. See README.md for the project scope
and LICENSE for the original code's MIT license.

Close the older running copy, then open XenoCapture.exe.
Keep the gpu folder beside the executable.
Click Play to start in the embedded preview.
Choose a monitor under Output > Fullscreen display, then press Fullscreen or F11.
Esc or F11 animates the fullscreen image into the embedded preview.
Smooth jagged edges in fullscreen is on by default under Output.
Use Feedback in the top bar to control animations, including the fullscreen pop, and interface sounds.
New recordings use the Xeno Capture folder in place of the old default folder.

Appearance: use Light mode / Dark mode in the top bar. Xeno Capture saves your choice when you close the app. On first launch it imports settings from the previous app name if available.

ANDROID OVER A USB DATA CABLE

Connect one Android phone, unlock it, and enable USB debugging under
Developer options. In Xeno Capture, open Input > Phone screen and select
Play Android over USB. Approve the debugging request on the phone if shown.
Leave Always allow from this computer unchecked for temporary approval.
Android USB keeps the scrcpy helper off-screen while Xeno shows the preview.
For live sound on Android 11 or newer, check Phone output before clicking
Play. This routes the phone's sound to the Windows playback output.
Use the Phone volume slider below the preview to adjust the mirroring volume.
Disconnect in Xeno Capture when finished, then turn off USB debugging on
the phone if you no longer need it. Keep the android folder beside the app.

EXPERIMENTAL AI UPSCALING (preview only, off by default)
Output > AI Video Super Resolution > enable the switch.
Choose Auto, 720p, 1080p, 1440p or 4K processing, then Performance,
Balanced, High or Ultra quality. High is the starting quality.
The status shows the actual enhanced dimensions. Aspect ratio is kept;
higher-resolution inputs are not reduced. A smaller window fits the
processed image back to its visible size.

The filter uses NVIDIA RTX Video Super Resolution on a compatible RTX GPU.
It temporarily takes priority over Standard / Normal / RTX Video.
Turning it off restores your selection. Recording/audio are independent.
First use initializes the model. Higher quality/resolution costs GPU work.

Fullscreen smooths short bursts of early source frames even with generation
off. An early frame can wait up to 2 ms. Late frames proceed immediately.

EXPERIMENTAL FRAME GENERATION (preview only, off by default)
To test smoother motion, choose Adaptive 2x, 3x or 4x frame generation.
Leave Force 30 FPS game cadence off for automatic content detection.
30 FPS games target 60 / 90 / 120 FPS; 60 FPS games target 120 / 180 / 240.
The screen must support the target, and the GPU must finish processing
within that interval. The counter separates shown, generated and input FPS.
Generation adds one content frame of buffering. Motion checks preserve
source frames on scene cuts and suppress interpolation when too much
high-contrast motion cannot be matched.

With frame generation off, the preview presents the latest available
frame as soon as the display path is ready. For lower GPU use, choose
Preview upscaling > Performance and leave AI Video Super Resolution off.

AI preview reuses the loaded model when targets change and between windowed
and fullscreen playback. Identical capture frames can reuse inference output.
See AI PREVIEW AND PACING REVIEW.md for tests and remaining limits.
Fast motion, HUD edges and scene changes can still show artifacts or repeats.
